### Title
Broken Proportionality: Prosecutorial Demands and State Financial Loss in Indonesian Corruption Cases

### Authors
Mukhlis Amien
Universitas Bhinneka Nusantara - Department of Informatics
Email: amien@ubhinus.ac.id

### Abstract (copy-paste this)
How do prosecutors calibrate sentencing demands to the scale of corruption? The proportionality principle requires that punishment severity reflect crime severity. We test this principle using 291 Indonesian Supreme Court corruption verdicts with documented state financial losses (kerugian negara) ranging from Rp 10 million to Rp 300 trillion. We find that prosecution demands (tuntutan) are strikingly inelastic to state loss magnitude: the log-log elasticity is only 0.109 (bootstrap 95% CI [0.078, 0.148]), meaning that a 100-fold increase in state financial loss is associated with only a 1.7-fold increase in prosecution demand. In concrete terms, a Rp 10 million corruption case receives a mean demand of 3.2 years, while a Rp 100 billion case — 10,000 times larger — receives only 8.8 years (2.7x more). The formal test of proportionality (H0: elasticity = 1.0) is rejected with p = 2.92e-197. Prosecution demands explain only 31.5% of sentencing variance compared to 60% for the demand-sentence relationship, indicating that prosecutors operate with greater discretion than judges. The finding is robust to heteroskedasticity-consistent standard errors (HC3 p = 6.53e-132), outlier removal, temporal subsamples, and alternative functional forms. These findings reveal a regressive feature of Indonesian corruption prosecution where the marginal prosecutorial response to additional state loss is near zero, creating negligible deterrence for escalating corruption.
